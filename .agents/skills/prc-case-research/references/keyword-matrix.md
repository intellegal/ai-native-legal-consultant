# Query Intent and Progressive Keyword Design

The research method is in `research-method.md`; this reference describes its query-design step. Use only the selected provider for executable syntax.

## Before and during keyword design

Start from the user's question, known facts, legal expressions, opposing account and available seed cases. Early retrieval helps discover the language actually used by courts. Its size and number of paths are task-dependent; do not require one or two minimal probes, and do not postpone all retrieval until a complete keyword plan exists.

Keep expressions provisional throughout the project. Read useful cases, distinguish the court's language from party allegations, appraisal/evidence text and database processing, and use the new wording and uncovered distinctions to redesign later queries. A fixed first keyword list is not the whole project plan.

## Intent map

Where helpful, record: the controlling question; factual/evidentiary or procedural relation to test; neutral expressions; competing characterization or burden; desired field/context; useful filters; current gaps; and why the next query can improve the answer. This is a compact part of the research record, not a mandatory separate matrix.

Include materially useful synonyms, historical expressions and alternate characterizations. Do not extend vocabulary only on the favored side, and do not treat a result word such as 支持 or 不予支持 as proof of the court's position on the target issue.

## Translate intent into source-specific expressions

- Follow the selected, supported provider's operational reference for connectors, fields and position controls. Exploit its actual capabilities rather than copying another database's expressions. Split unsupported combinations into justified paths; inspect raw context when exclusion or proximity cannot be expressed, and disclose non-equivalence.
- Use user-specified necessary filters from the outset. Calibrate additional noise filters and check that they have not removed relevant boundary cases.

## Next iteration

Tighten repeatable noise, broaden learned language, test a competing explanation, fill a missing coverage cell, deepen a productive path or close one that no longer adds useful information. Several justified paths may run within the research plan; there is no fixed number or universal scoring threshold.

Record the material change and next decision in the research record. Candidate breadth, full-text learning, viewpoint comparison, identity checks and known-case recovery feed the next round. Stop or continue under the shared quality test, not a requested batch size.
